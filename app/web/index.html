Reading the configuration.

A precedent can match your query perfectly and still be unsafe to cite. The statute it applied may have been replaced, or a later bench may have overruled it. LexShift puts statutory continuity and judicial treatment inside the ranking itself, and shows the sentence behind every status.

Three rules the ranking does not break.

They are written into the scoring, not added afterwards.

Old precedents still count

Old precedents still count.

The BNS replaced the IPC on 1 July 2024, but a BNS query still retrieves IPC-era judgments. Continuity lowers a result only in proportion to how much the law changed.

No keyword matching for treatment.

Only the sentences around a specific cited case are classified. Words such as “overruled” in an objection, or “set aside” on appeal, are not an overruling of a precedent.

Newer is not stronger.

Recency never raises or lowers a score. Bench strength and treatment do, because a smaller bench cannot overrule a larger one.
